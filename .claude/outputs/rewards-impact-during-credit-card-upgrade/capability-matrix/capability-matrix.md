## Why they likely did it that way

- Chase Ultimate Rewards — automatic continuity: points are one shared
  currency/ledger across all Ultimate Rewards-earning cards, so a product
  change re-labels the card on the account without touching the underlying
  points balance — there's nothing for an ops team to reconcile.
- Chase Ultimate Rewards — redemption value gated by tier, not balance: the
  transfer-partner network and boosted portal redemption rate are funded by
  premium-card annual fees, so Chase ties those redemption options to
  whichever card is currently held rather than to the points themselves,
  protecting that fee-supported revenue model while still letting the
  balance move freely.
- American Express Membership Rewards — same-family-only upgrades: cash
  back is a direct monetary rebate liability tracked in dollars, while
  Membership Rewards points are a separate proprietary loyalty currency
  with its own transfer-partner and breakage economics; crossing between
  them would force Amex to define and defend a conversion rate between two
  differently-modeled liabilities, so it avoids that by hard-walling
  upgrades within one currency family.
- Citi ThankYou Rewards / AAdvantage cards — same-family-only product
  changes: AAdvantage miles on co-branded cards are issued under a
  partnership agreement with American Airlines rather than being Citi's
  own currency, while ThankYou Points are Citi's proprietary currency;
  letting a customer cross that boundary would require Citi to either buy
  back miles already committed to the airline partner or invent a
  conversion rate between two very differently-costed currencies, so Citi
  keeps the two families walled off.
- Capital One Rewards — one-directional, self-service, fixed-rate
  conversion: making the cash-back-to-miles bridge self-service,
  one-directional, and fixed-rate lets Capital One offer more
  cross-currency flexibility than Amex or Citi allow, without opening a
  two-way arbitrage loophole or requiring an ops team to manually
  reconcile balances on every card change.
