## 2. Executive Summary

Two decisions anchor this project. First,
`decision-2026-08-10-generalized-platform-feature` recommends building a
generalized platform feature that automates reward point migration for
any credit card variant change — upgrade or downgrade — replacing the
current manual, Ops-driven process. Its rationale: the user explicitly
wants a feature that works for any card upgrade and confirmed downgrade
is in scope too, not a one-off fix for a single card pair or direction,
in order to remove Ops from the manual debit/credit loop.

Second, `decision-2026-08-10-conversion-rounding-precision-rule`
recommends defining the platform's point-conversion precision rule as
round-half-to-even to the nearest whole point, combined with a
per-client rounding-remainder tracking record for periodic finance
reconciliation. Its rationale: no precision rule existed anywhere,
leaving the exact-reconciliation requirement's "exact...within the
method's defined precision" clause unsatisfiable, and round-half-to-even
removes the directional bias a simpler round-half-up rule would still
carry, while remainder tracking makes aggregate drift empirically
observable rather than an unverified assumption of neutrality.

## 3. Problem Statement

Per `insight-2026-08-10-manual-points-migration`: today, when a customer
moves to a higher credit card variant, Ops manually debits their reward
points balance on the old card and credits the new card using a
conversion ratio — there is no automated platform handling of this. Per
`insight-2026-08-10-roadmap-and-scaling-driver`, this is a committed
platform roadmap item, driven by Ops not scaling with growing
upgrade/downgrade volume. Per `insight-2026-08-10-migration-volume`, Ops
currently processes roughly 500 of these migrations manually per month,
expected to grow into the thousands as the platform scales — a volume
Ops cannot handle manually.

### `decision-2026-08-10-generalized-platform-feature`
**Title:** Build a generalized platform feature for reward migration on
card upgrade or downgrade, not a per-case fix.
**Status:** active, confidence: medium.

**Alternatives considered:**
1. *Continue current manual Ops process (debit old card, credit new
   card per conversion ratio, handled case-by-case).*
   Pros: no build cost; already working today. Cons: doesn't scale
   across all card variant pairs; manual, so slower and error-prone;
   Ops-dependent for every upgrade or downgrade. Rejected because: the
   user wants a platform capability that works for any credit card
   variant change, not a per-pair manual workaround.
2. *Build a generalized platform feature that automates reward
   migration for any credit card upgrade or downgrade.* Pros: scales to
   any card variant pair, either direction, without new Ops work per
   pair; removes manual handling and its error surface. Cons: requires
   defining conversion ratio logic generically across all variant pairs
   and both directions; scope and edge cases not yet fully known.

**Recommendation:** Build a platform feature that automates reward
point migration for any credit card variant change, upgrade or
downgrade, replacing the manual Ops process.

**Rationale:** The user explicitly stated the goal is a platform
feature that works for any credit card upgrade, and confirmed downgrade
is in scope too — not a one-off fix for a single card pair or
direction, to remove Ops from the manual debit/credit loop.

**Open questions:** none logged.

### `decision-2026-08-10-conversion-rounding-precision-rule`
**Title:** Adopt round-half-to-even plus per-client remainder tracking
as the platform's point-conversion rounding rule.
**Status:** active, confidence: medium. Tags include
`strategy-recommendation`.

**Alternatives considered:**
1. *Round-half-up to nearest whole point, applied uniformly.* Pros:
   simplest rule to implement and explain; directly defines "exact...
   within the method's defined precision." Cons: ties always round
   upward (a directional bias baked into the rule); no mechanism to
   detect or verify that fractional remainders are actually close to
   evenly distributed at scale — near-neutrality is an assumption, not
   a measured fact. Rejected because: leaves
   `risk-2026-08-10-systematic-rounding-bias` unaddressed at the
   monitoring level — it assumes aggregate drift is small without any
   way to catch it if that assumption is wrong.
   Impact: customer impact — ties round in customer's favor, neutral-
   to-slightly-positive; business impact — simplest to communicate, but
   assumes (strategy-agent's judgment, not evidence) remainders are
   close to uniformly distributed, never verified; engineering
   complexity: low; operational complexity: low; scalability risk —
   undetected minor bias compounds as volume grows into the thousands,
   with no monitoring in place; long-term maintainability — simple, but
   leaves the aggregate-drift question permanently unanswered.
2. *Round down (truncate), always in the platform's favor.* Pros:
   simplest possible rule; guarantees the platform never over-credits;
   easy to audit. Cons: deterministically realizes the systematic bias
   risk describes as hypothetical, in the customer-unfavorable
   direction, on every fractional migration; every fractional migration
   credits strictly less than the exact computed value, directly
   feeding the customer-dispute risk. Rejected because: this doesn't
   merely risk the systematic bias risk flags — it guarantees it, from
   day one, at full volume, in the direction least favorable to
   customers.
   Impact: customer impact — always strictly less than exact computed
   value, consistently "short"; business impact — guarantees no
   over-crediting but (strategy-agent's judgment, not evidence) risks
   being read as systematically extracting value from customers if
   noticed; engineering complexity: low; operational complexity: low;
   scalability risk — realizes the systematic-bias risk deterministically
   at full volume; long-term maintainability — simple technically, but
   the aggregate customer-unfavorable drift only grows with volume.
3. *Extend the points ledger to fractional precision, eliminating
   rounding at migration time entirely.* Pros: no point value is ever
   lost or gained at migration — satisfies exactness most literally;
   removes the rounding-bias risk entirely at the migration step. Cons:
   (strategy-agent's judgment, not evidence) likely just relocates the
   same undefined-precision problem to redemption/display time, which
   no requirement in this store scopes; touches the core points ledger
   data model and every downstream consumer, none confirmed to already
   support fractional values. Rejected because: trades a scoped,
   definable rounding-rule decision for an open-ended ledger-precision
   migration with unscoped downstream blast radius, and doesn't
   actually eliminate the need for a rounding rule — it just moves the
   problem.
   Impact: customer impact — literal computed value always shown;
   business impact — removes the exposure at migration step but likely
   relocates it; engineering complexity: high (touches ledger core data
   model and every downstream consumer, including the new audit
   record); operational complexity: medium (no new review queue, but
   Ops/Support must explain fractional balances); scalability risk —
   low ongoing once built, but the initial change itself is a scale
   risk given projected volume growth; long-term maintainability — most
   durable fix specifically, but a much larger, less-scoped surface
   area for a problem that may not have needed full ledger precision.
4. *(Chosen)* Round-half-to-even (banker's rounding) to the nearest
   whole point, combined with a per-client rounding-remainder tracking
   record for periodic finance reconciliation. Pros: removes the
   directional tie-bias Option 1 has, closest to a statistically
   neutral per-migration rule without extending ledger precision;
   directly answers the undefined "precision" clause with a specific,
   standard, auditable rule; remainder-tracking makes aggregate drift
   observable and reconcilable rather than assuming neutrality — directly
   targets the systematic-bias risk's actual concern. Cons: adds a new
   deliverable (the remainder-tracking record/report) beyond a rounding
   rule alone; (strategy-agent's judgment, not evidence) the
   remainder-tracking mechanism's owner and reconciliation cadence are
   not yet defined by any requirement in this store.
   Impact: customer impact — standard, statistically neutral rounding
   with no directional favoritism, reducing the chance a dispute
   reflects a systematic pattern; business impact — defines the
   undefined precision clause and gives Finance a concrete per-client
   remainder record, turning the systematic-bias risk from an
   unmonitored hypothetical into a measured, reviewable quantity;
   engineering complexity: medium (rounding rule itself is low, but the
   remainder-tracking mechanism is new logic beyond what any current
   requirement scopes); operational complexity: medium (new artifact
   needing a defined owner and cadence, similar in kind to the
   exception queue in `system-impact-2026-08-10-conversion-exception-queue`,
   though this decision doesn't itself assign that owner); scalability
   risk — directly targets the systematic-bias risk as volume grows,
   making emerging drift visible and reconcilable before it becomes a
   large, undetected accounting discrepancy; long-term maintainability
   — adds one recurring operational process that must be sustained, but
   resolves both the per-migration precision question and the
   aggregate-drift risk in the same decision, unlike Options 1-3.

**Recommendation:** Define the platform's point-conversion precision
rule as round-half-to-even to the nearest whole point, and add a
per-client rounding-remainder tracking record that accumulates the
difference between each migration's exact computed value and its
rounded credited amount, for periodic finance reconciliation.

**Rationale:** Directly resolves
`system-impact-2026-08-10-conversion-rounding-precision` (no precision
rule was defined anywhere, leaving the exact-reconciliation
requirement's clause unsatisfiable) by naming round-half-to-even as
that rule. It also directly addresses
`risk-2026-08-10-systematic-rounding-bias`, which is specifically about
aggregate, portfolio-level drift, not just per-migration exactness:
round-half-to-even removes the one directional bias source a simpler
round-half-up rule would still carry, and the added remainder-tracking
gives Finance a way to detect drift empirically rather than relying on
an unverified assumption of even distribution — an assumption none of
Options 1-3 actually validate, they only differ in whether that
assumption's failure would be visible. This also reduces the
customer-dispute risk's likelihood indirectly: with no built-in
directional favoritism, a customer's dispute is more likely a one-off
or a genuine config error the audit trail can investigate, rather than
a symptom of a systematic shortfall, unlike Option 2's guaranteed
under-crediting. The recommendation rests on one unvalidated assumption
of its own, disclosed rather than presented as settled: that a
remainder-tracking mechanism at the per-client level is buildable
within the feature's existing scope and doesn't require the larger
ledger-precision change of Option 3 — that scoping hasn't been
validated against solution-design's actual implementation.

## 20. Functional Requirements

All five requirements elaborate
`decision-2026-08-10-generalized-platform-feature`:

1. **`requirement-2026-08-10-automatic-migration-trigger`** — The
   system automatically initiates a reward points migration whenever
   the existing card-variant-change event fires, for both upgrades and
   downgrades, without requiring manual Ops action.
2. **`requirement-2026-08-10-configurable-conversion-method`** — For
   each card variant pair, the client configures which conversion
   method applies — fixed percentage, equal point count, or equal
   point value — and the migration applies whichever method is
   configured for that exact pair.
3. **`requirement-2026-08-10-exact-points-reconciliation`** — After a
   migration completes, the points debited from the old card equal its
   full pre-migration balance, and the points credited to the new card
   equal exactly the value the client's configured conversion method
   computes from that debited amount.
4. **`requirement-2026-08-10-same-day-processing-sla`** — Every points
   migration completes within the same business day the triggering
   card-variant-change event was published.
5. **`requirement-2026-08-10-migration-audit-trail`** — Every points
   migration produces a retrievable record of the pre-migration
   balance, post-migration balance, the specific conversion method and
   parameters applied, and the completion timestamp.

## 21. Acceptance Criteria

Grouped under `decision-2026-08-10-generalized-platform-feature`:

**`requirement-2026-08-10-automatic-migration-trigger`**
- When the internal system publishes a card-variant-change event for a
  customer (upgrade or downgrade), the platform initiates a points
  migration for that customer without any manual Ops step.
- Every published card-variant-change event results in either a
  completed migration or a flagged failure requiring review — none are
  silently dropped.

**`requirement-2026-08-10-configurable-conversion-method`**
- A client can configure, per card variant pair, exactly one method: a
  fixed conversion percentage, an equal-point-count method, or an
  equal-point-value method.
- When a migration runs for a given card variant pair, it applies the
  method configured for that exact pair rather than a platform-wide
  default.
- Two different clients can configure different conversion methods for
  otherwise-identical card variant pairs without one client's
  configuration affecting the other's.

**`requirement-2026-08-10-exact-points-reconciliation`**
- The old card's point balance after migration is zero (its full
  pre-migration balance was debited).
- The new card's credited amount matches the value computed by
  applying the configured conversion method to the debited amount, with
  no rounding or calculation discrepancy beyond the method's defined
  precision.
- Given the same starting balance and the same configured method,
  repeating the calculation produces the identical credited amount
  every time.

**`requirement-2026-08-10-same-day-processing-sla`**
- For a card-variant-change event published on a given business day,
  the corresponding migration (old card debited, new card credited)
  completes before that business day ends.
- Migration is not required to complete in real time or within minutes
  of the event; same-business-day completion satisfies this
  requirement.

**`requirement-2026-08-10-migration-audit-trail`**
- For any completed migration, the pre-migration balance, post-
  migration balance, the conversion method and parameters applied (e.g.
  which percentage, or which method type), and the completion timestamp
  can be retrieved by card or customer identifier.
- The audit record is created and retained at migration time,
  independent of whether a dispute is ever raised — not generated
  on-demand only after a dispute occurs.

## 26. System Impact Analysis

All 8 `system-impact` records, grouped by `system_area`:

**monitoring**
- `system-impact-2026-08-10-silent-event-drop-detection` (severity:
  high) — elaborates `requirement-2026-08-10-automatic-migration-
  trigger`. A card-variant-change event that fails before migration
  processing even starts (consumer outage, dropped message) looks
  identical to a day with no card variant changes at all, unless the
  platform tracks event-received counts against migration-completed-
  or-flagged counts and alerts on any gap — that comparison doesn't
  exist today because Ops previously processed each case by hand and
  would notice via a customer calling in, not a systemic count
  mismatch.
- `system-impact-2026-08-10-sla-breach-visibility` (severity: medium) —
  elaborates `requirement-2026-08-10-same-day-processing-sla`. Because
  migration is asynchronous and completes any time within a business
  day rather than immediately per event, the platform needs to
  distinguish a migration still within today's processing window from
  one about to breach the same-day deadline — a pending-migration age
  metric that doesn't exist today since Ops closed each case
  synchronously by hand.

**operations**
- `system-impact-2026-08-10-conversion-exception-queue` (severity:
  medium) — elaborates `requirement-2026-08-10-automatic-migration-
  trigger` and `requirement-2026-08-10-configurable-conversion-method`.
  Today Ops manually processes every migration, so there's no separate
  "missing configuration" failure mode to route anywhere. Once
  migration is automated, any card variant pair a client hasn't
  configured a method for (e.g., a newly launched variant) falls into a
  flagged-for-review state with no defined owner, queue, or SLA of its
  own — distinct from the general migration process it's replacing.

**data-model**
- `system-impact-2026-08-10-conversion-config-data-model` (severity:
  medium) — elaborates `requirement-2026-08-10-configurable-conversion-
  method`. Storing "which method, with which parameters, applies to
  card variant pair X for client Y" is new structured configuration —
  today this exists only as a person's judgment call per manual case,
  not a queryable data model.
- `system-impact-2026-08-10-conversion-rounding-precision` (severity:
  high) — elaborates `requirement-2026-08-10-exact-points-
  reconciliation` and `requirement-2026-08-10-configurable-conversion-
  method`. A fixed-percentage method applied to an arbitrary
  pre-migration balance often produces a non-integer result (e.g. 233
  points at 70% = 163.1), but no precision or rounding rule is defined
  anywhere in the requirements or the existing points ledger, so
  "exact" is currently undefined for any non-whole-number outcome.
  (Note: this gap is what `decision-2026-08-10-conversion-rounding-
  precision-rule`, Section 11, resolves.)
- `system-impact-2026-08-10-audit-record-data-model` (severity: medium)
  — elaborates `requirement-2026-08-10-migration-audit-trail`. The
  audit requirement needs the specific conversion method and parameters
  applied captured per migration and retained regardless of whether a
  dispute occurs — metadata a balance-tracking points ledger
  transaction entry doesn't inherently carry, so a new record type
  keyed by migration needs to exist.

**customer-support**
- `system-impact-2026-08-10-cs-audit-lookup-access` (severity: high) —
  elaborates `requirement-2026-08-10-migration-audit-trail`. The
  requirement guarantees the record is retrievable by identifier, but
  not who can retrieve it. Today a dispute over a manual migration
  would be resolved by Ops, who performed the migration and had direct
  system access; once migration is automated, the customer support
  agent who actually fields the dispute call has no defined tool or
  permission path to look up that record unless one is built, leaving
  the data retrievable in principle but not by the people who need it
  to answer a customer in the moment.

**customer-communication**
- `system-impact-2026-08-10-no-customer-notification` (severity:
  medium) — elaborates `requirement-2026-08-10-automatic-migration-
  trigger`. None of the five requirements specify notifying the
  customer when a migration completes. The manual Ops process at least
  involved a person touching the account on the day of the change; full
  automation removes that without replacing it with any customer-facing
  signal, so a customer's points balance changing via an unannounced
  automated debit-then-credit is exactly the condition
  `risk-2026-08-10-customer-dispute` already identifies as the primary
  risk — this gap is a direct driver of that risk's likelihood,
  independent of whether the calculation itself is correct.

## 27. Risks & Mitigations

**`risk-2026-08-10-customer-dispute`** (confidence: medium) — Customer
disputes over migrated point amount. Reward points carry real value to
customers, so an incorrect or unclear migration amount is expected to
surface as a dispute.

Cross-check — several records address aspects of this risk, but nothing
in the store closes it:
- `requirement-2026-08-10-exact-points-reconciliation` targets
  preventing the dispute condition itself: its own text states
  "Exactness here is what determines whether
  `risk-2026-08-10-customer-dispute` materializes, since a mismatch
  between the expected and received amount is the disputed condition
  itself."
- `requirement-2026-08-10-migration-audit-trail` explicitly does **not**
  claim to prevent disputes — its own text says: "This requirement
  doesn't attempt to prevent disputes — that's what
  `requirement-2026-08-10-exact-points-reconciliation` targets — it
  makes disputes that do occur investigable."
- `system-impact-2026-08-10-cs-audit-lookup-access` identifies that
  even the audit trail's investigability is incomplete in practice:
  support agents have no defined access path, "leaving the data
  retrievable in principle but not by the people who need it to answer
  a customer in the moment."
- `system-impact-2026-08-10-no-customer-notification` identifies a gap
  that increases this risk's likelihood, not one that reduces it: "this
  gap is a direct driver of that risk's likelihood, independent of
  whether the calculation itself is correct."
- `decision-2026-08-10-conversion-rounding-precision-rule`'s rationale
  claims an indirect reduction: with round-half-to-even's lack of
  directional favoritism, "a customer's dispute about their credited
  amount is more likely a one-off or a genuine config error the audit
  trail...can investigate, rather than a symptom of a systematic
  shortfall."

No record in the store claims this risk is resolved, and this schema
has no `mitigation` record type — the above is evidence that several
requirements and one decision were reasoned around this risk, with at
least two records (`migration-audit-trail`, `no-customer-notification`)
explicitly disclosing where that reasoning stops short of closing it.

**`risk-2026-08-10-systematic-rounding-bias`** (confidence: low) —
Undefined rounding direction on fractional point conversions could
create a systematic, portfolio-level point-liability discrepancy,
distinct from any single customer's dispute. Per its own `details`:
"requirement-2026-08-10-exact-points-reconciliation defines exactness
per-migration but says nothing about aggregate drift across migrations,
and no rounding direction is specified anywhere in the requirements to
date." At current/projected volume (~500/month growing into the
thousands, `insight-2026-08-10-migration-volume`), "a one-directional
rounding rule would compound into a portfolio-level point-liability
discrepancy...a finance/accounting exposure separate from any
individual customer's dispute."

Cross-check — this risk is the one most substantively addressed in the
store: `decision-2026-08-10-conversion-rounding-precision-rule` names
this risk directly in `related_ids` and its rationale states it
"directly addresses `risk-2026-08-10-systematic-rounding-bias`,"
proposing round-half-to-even rounding plus per-client remainder
tracking specifically to make aggregate drift "empirically" observable
"rather than relying on an unverified assumption that fractional
remainders are evenly distributed across migrations." However, this is
a decision to build a mechanism, not proof the mechanism exists or is
operating: that same decision's own open questions state the
reconciliation cadence and owner (Finance vs Ops) are "not defined by
this decision," and whether the remainder-tracking record is even "a
new requirement solution-design needs to scope separately" is "not
resolved here." No requirement record in the store yet implements the
remainder-tracking mechanism. This risk therefore has a proposed
approach, not a confirmed mitigation.
